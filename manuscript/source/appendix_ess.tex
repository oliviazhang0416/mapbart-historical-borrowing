\section{Prior effective sample size: comparison, derivation and computation}\label{sec:B}

\subsection{Comparison with established prior-ESS definitions}\label{sec:B.1}
Table~\ref{tab:ESScomparison} places the proposed definition beside the variance calibration of \citet{Neuenschwander2010MAP}, the curvature match of \citet{Morita2008ESS}, and the expected local-information-ratio (ELIR) definition of \citet{neuenschwander2020}. Throughout the table, $\theta_h$ denotes a historical-study parameter or estimand, with $h$ indexing historical studies when applicable, and $\theta_\star$ denotes the corresponding current-study parameter or estimand. The two Neuenschwander rows separate the complete-pooling calibration anchor from the partial-pooling MAP prior whose ESS is inferred. For Morita's method, write $i_\epsilon(\theta_\star)\coloneqq-\partial_{\theta_\star}^2\log\pi_\epsilon(\theta_\star)$ for low-information-prior curvature and $j_m(Y_{1:m};\theta_\star)\coloneqq-\partial_{\theta_\star}^2\log\{\prod_{r=1}^m f(Y_r\mid\theta_\star)\}$ for observed likelihood information. For the proposed method, $(\theta_h,\delta_\star,\theta_\star)$ corresponds to $(\mu_f,\mu_g,\mu)$ in the main text.

Neuenschwander's complete-pooling prior supplies a known ESS anchor rather than a hypothetical-likelihood reference. Morita's reference object is a posterior constructed from a same-mean low-information prior and hypothetical data. ELIR and the proposed method use one-observation Fisher information directly. ELIR averages local curvature ratios over the target prior; the proposed method averages conditional inverse-variance matches over the calibration distribution of $\tau_0^2$. Consequently, the proposed ESS does not inherit ELIR's predictive-consistency property \citep{neuenschwander2020}.

\subsection{Calculation of historical uncertainty}\label{sec:B.2}
For the main-text target $\mu_f=n_2^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_2}f(x_{2,i})$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Vf}
V_f
=\frac1{n_2^2}
\sum_{i=1}^{n_2}\sum_{i'=1}^{n_2}
\Cov\{f(x_{2,i}),f(x_{2,i'})
\mid\mathbf Y_3,\mathbf X_3,\mathbf X_2\}.
\end{equation}
Given preliminary posterior draws $f^{(b)}$, form
$\mu_f^{(b)}\coloneqq n_2^{-1}\sum_i f^{(b)}(x_{2,i})$ and estimate $V_f$ by their sample variance. This calculation integrates tree and leaf uncertainty and retains cross-profile covariance. Averaging the pointwise posterior variances would omit the covariance terms.

\subsection{Derivation of discrepancy uncertainty}\label{sec:B.3}
Let $\mathcal T^g\coloneqq(\mathcal T_{h^g}^g)_{h^g=1}^{H_g}$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weights}
a_{h^g\ell}\coloneqq
\frac1{n_2}\sum_{i=1}^{n_2}
\ind\{x_{2,i}\in\ell\text{ of tree }h^g\}.
\end{equation}
Regrouping the target-profile average of the discrepancy trees gives
\begin{equation}
\mu_g=\sum_{h^g,\ell}a_{h^g\ell}\theta^g_{h^g\ell}.
\end{equation}
Within each tree, $a_{h^g\ell}\geq0$ and $\sum_\ell a_{h^g\ell}=1$. Conditional on the trees and indicators, the independent zero-mean leaf priors imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditionalVg}
\begin{aligned}
\E(\mu_g\mid\mathcal T^g,\mathbf z,w,\tau_0^2)&=0,\\
\Var(\mu_g\mid\mathcal T^g,\mathbf z,w,\tau_0^2)
&=\sum_{h^g,\ell}a_{h^g\ell}^2
\{z_{h^g\ell}\tau_0^2+(1-z_{h^g\ell})\tau_1^2\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The conditional mean remains zero after integrating over the indicators, so the between-mean term in the law of total variance vanishes. Since $\E(z_{h^g\ell}\mid w)=w$,
\[
\Var(\mu_g\mid\mathcal T^g,w,\tau_0^2)
=\{w\tau_0^2+(1-w)\tau_1^2\}
\sum_{h^g,\ell}a_{h^g\ell}^2.
\]
The same argument applies when integrating over the prior trees, giving main-text equation~\ref{T-eq:Vgmain}. Thus $V_g$ integrates leaf, indicator and tree uncertainty at fixed $w,\tau_0^2$. The identity is exact under the stated independent zero-mean priors; Monte Carlo enters only in evaluating the tree expectation.

\subsection{Proof of the prior ESS curve theorem}\label{sec:B.4}
Under the all-spike calibration, let
\[
C_g\coloneqq
\E_{\mathcal T^g}\!\left[\sum_{h^g,\ell}a_{h^g\ell}^2\right].
\]
Every finite tree has at least one occupied terminal node and
$\sum_\ell a_{h^g\ell}^2\leq1$, so $0<C_g\leq H_g$. If
$U\sim\chi^2_{\nu_0}$, the scaled inverse-chi-square variable can be coupled over all scale values as $\tau_0^2=\nu_0s_0^2/U$. Main-text equation~\ref{T-eq:essspike} becomes
\[
\ESS_{\tau_0}(s_0^2)
=\E_U\!\left[
\frac{\sigma_2^2}{V_f+C_g\nu_0s_0^2/U}
\right].
\]
For every $U>0$, the integrand is continuous, strictly decreasing and strictly convex in $s_0^2>0$, and is bounded above by $\sigma_2^2/V_f$. Dominated convergence establishes continuity and the limits
\[
\lim_{s_0^2\downarrow0}\ESS_{\tau_0}(s_0^2)
=\frac{\sigma_2^2}{V_f},
\qquad
\lim_{s_0^2\to\infty}\ESS_{\tau_0}(s_0^2)=0.
\]
Pointwise strict inequalities preserve strict monotonicity and strict convexity after expectation. The intermediate value theorem and strict monotonicity give a unique positive solution for each target in $(0,\sigma_2^2/V_f)$. This proves main-text Theorem~\ref{T-thm:ess} without requiring $\E(\tau_0^2)$ to be finite.

\subsection{Monte Carlo evaluation and implemented prior-scale selection}\label{sec:B.5}
Estimate $V_f$ from the preliminary posterior target averages and estimate
$C_g$ by simulating discrepancy trees from the calibration prior. For independent
$U_b\sim\chi^2_{\nu_0}$, $b=1,\ldots,B$, evaluate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MCess}
\widehat\ESS_{\tau_0}(s_0^2)
\coloneqq\frac1B\sum_{b=1}^B
\frac{\widehat\sigma_2^2}
{\widehat V_f+(\nu_0s_0^2/U_b)\widehat C_g}.
\end{equation}
Reusing the $U_b$ values over the scale grid makes the Monte Carlo curve monotone. The preliminary $f$ predictions are posterior draws conditional on the historical dataset; the computation does not repeatedly generate outcome datasets.

\noindent\textbf{Algorithm B1: full-variance ESS evaluation and target selection.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Specify the current-control target profiles, historical outcome likelihood, candidate splitting rules and fixed reference residual variance estimate.
\item Fit the historical model, average each posterior prediction draw over the target profiles and estimate $V_f$.
\item Simulate discrepancy trees from the calibration prior and estimate $C_g$.
\item Draw $U_b\sim\chi^2_{\nu_0}$ and calculate \eqref{eq:MCess} over candidate scales.
\item For an attainable target, solve the monotone equation or select a grid approximation; record targets outside the attainable range.
\end{enumerate}

The implemented selector divides the historical target-mean draws into $J=20$ consecutive blocks, calculates a variance in each block and rescales the block variances so that their arithmetic mean equals the variance across all draws. Write the rescaled values as $\widehat V_{f,k}$. It substitutes each $\widehat V_{f,k}$ into \eqref{eq:MCess}, yielding $\widehat\ESS_k(s_0^2)$, and uses their arithmetic mean as the central curve. With
$m_*\coloneqq\min(m_{\mathrm{requested}},\widehat\sigma_2^2/\widehat V_f)$, the selector chooses the smallest supplied scale satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockrule}
\frac1J\sum_{k=1}^J
\ind\{\widehat\ESS_k(s_0^2)\leq m_*\}\geq0.95.
\end{equation}
If no grid value satisfies the rule, it chooses the largest value. If the requested target reaches or exceeds the full-variance ceiling, the current implementation chooses the smallest grid value.

The block-based central curve does not generally have the full-variance ceiling as its zero-scale limit because
\[
\lim_{s_0^2\downarrow0}
\frac1J\sum_{k=1}^J\widehat\ESS_k(s_0^2)
=\frac1J\sum_{k=1}^J
\frac{\widehat\sigma_2^2}{\widehat V_{f,k}}
\geq\frac{\widehat\sigma_2^2}{\widehat V_f},
\]
with equality only when the positive block variances are equal. Consecutive blocks of an autocorrelated Markov chain also do not supply a formal 95\% posterior or repeated-sampling guarantee. Figure S1 illustrates the curves for one Gaussian two-arm dataset; Table S11 evaluates the full-variance expression in the application.

\subsection{Pointwise, subgroup and overall targets}\label{sec:B.6}
For one target profile $x$, one terminal node in each discrepancy tree has weight one, so
\[
V_g(w,\tau_0^2;x)
=H_g\{w\tau_0^2+(1-w)\tau_1^2\}.
\]
For a subgroup target-profile set $\mathbf X_G$, retaining multiplicities, let
$n_G\coloneqq|\mathbf X_G|$ and define
\[
a_{h^g\ell}(G)\coloneqq
n_G^{-1}\sum_{x\in\mathbf X_G}
\ind\{x\in\ell\text{ of tree }h^g\}.
\]
$V_f(G)$ is the variance across historical-posterior averages over $\mathbf X_G$, and $V_g(w,\tau_0^2;G)$ follows main-text equation~\ref{T-eq:Vgmain} after replacing $a_{h^g\ell}$ by $a_{h^g\ell}(G)$. The subgroup ESS is obtained from main-text equation~\ref{T-eq:essgeneral}; it is the ESS of the subgroup mean, rather than an average of individual-profile ESS values.

For a partition with weights $p_G$, the overall mean is $\mu=\sum_Gp_G\mu_G$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonadd}
\Var(\mu)
=\sum_Gp_G^2\Var(\mu_G)
+\sum_{G\ne G'}p_Gp_{G'}\Cov(\mu_G,\mu_{G'}).
\end{equation}
The same fitted functions inform several groups, so the covariance terms generally do not vanish. Inverse variance is also nonlinear. Subgroup or individual-profile ESS values therefore do not generally sum or average to the overall ESS.

\subsection{Reference interpretation and implementation qualifications}\label{sec:B.7}
The normal reference uses a fixed $\sigma_2^2$. Numerical calibration substitutes a fixed estimate $\widehat\sigma_2^2$ and does not average over final-model posterior draws. In two-arm analyses this estimate uses randomized controls and can therefore depend on their outcomes even though $V_f$ does not. For a single-arm analysis, $\mathbf X_2$ is empty, so the ESS definition requires a separately prespecified target profile set. If historical residual variance is used as the reference scale, the additional assumption $\sigma_2^2=\sigma_3^2$ must be stated.

For survival outcomes, censoring enters the preliminary historical likelihood and therefore affects $V_f$. The reference remains one uncensored normal observation on the log-time scale. Its ESS cannot be interpreted as the number of patients in a trial with a specified censoring distribution without a separate calibration.

The preliminary historical fit, calibration tree simulation and final joint fit can use different admissible split rules. The current calibration simulator limits depth to 12 and does not impose the final two-arm requirement of at least five randomized controls per discrepancy leaf. Moreover, prior ESS calibration uses an untruncated spike-variance distribution, whereas the two-arm sampler uses its upper-truncated counterpart and single-arm fits fix the spike variance at $\min(s_0^2,\tau_1^2/2)$. The reported prior ESS is therefore a design and calibration summary for the stated target and reference, rather than the exact variance-matched ESS of the final fitting prior or a post-fit compatibility measure.
